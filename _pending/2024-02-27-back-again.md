It seems every ten years or so I restart my blog, run it for a few years, then stop. I think that's fine. The world keeps changing, stays interesting, and the ebb-and-flow of my blogging mostly seems to depend on what is happening elsewhere in my life. I'm currently at a point where I can take a break and collect my thoughts.

I last restarted my blog in 2014 with "The world changed a bit." The shifts in the last ten years have been even more impactful. Scala has had a rise, fall, and is now in its revival. Working from home went from a quiet agreement to the default during the pandemic. 

Some things remain consistent. Striving to make my code explicitly clear has made a tremendous difference. Someone referenced the _Understanding Service-Oriented Architecture_ artcile I wrote for java.net (RIP) back in 2006; it still rings true so I'll be reposting that soon.

My professional life has been very stable; I've spent most of my time leading and writing Scala back-end code for a distributed medical records project - SHRINE. I've almost finished SHRINE for HMS Catalyst - at least my part of getting it to work. I'll be writing some parts of how we did it. SHRINE is distributed across a network of hospitals; it uses message-oriented middleware, CQRS to track state, and a backwards/forwards compatible protocol. We also used a fine-tuned software development process that just flowed. Now that it is almost done I can write about what worked and what didn't with some perspective.

My older daughter is in high school. My younger daughter is a 5th grader. We use Scala to program Lego Ev3 robots for FIRST Lego League; the team did a presentation at NE Scala last October. I'll be writing about just how profound it is that Scala scales with experience, and how to put rules in place to bound the complexity of code.

I am also seeing some patterns now that I haven't before. I've made a career of helping smart people make systems work. I'm working on an essay about how to build reliable systems from unreliable parts which reaches all the way back to the flying robots at Alphatech, maybe further.



I miss the challenge of writing, so adding the blog back feels natural. I'll mostly be describing what I find while I blunder through hobby projects. Many others are learning Scala, so hopefully what I have to say will be of some benefit to them (you?). The blog will be fairly technical, with lots of code examples. I'll also be dropping in my opinion of what I think works well. I've been learning Scala largely in isolation, so I'll occasionally post something alien-looking. Feel especially free to call that out in the comments.
